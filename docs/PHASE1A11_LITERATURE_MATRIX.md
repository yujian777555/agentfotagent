## Cross-candidate conclusion

### Boundary-repair family
The accessible GECCO paper explicitly defines the core operators:
- **Whole-vector resampling:** repeat mutation / random-index selection until the mutant is feasible, with a maximum retry cap and projection fallback.
- **Random reinitialization:** independently sample each infeasible component uniformly inside the bounds.
- **Reflection:** reflect an infeasible coordinate over the violated boundary, repeating if it crosses the opposite bound.

Therefore:
- `repair_aware_resampling` is directly covered at the core-operator level.
- `repair-aware-mutation-resample` is a known resampling method plus a conditional threshold and F shrink; this is at best a minor combination until a distinct mechanism is demonstrated.
- `repair-aware-mutation-reflect` combines known reflection/resampling with a repair-flag multiplier on success-history credit. The targeted search did not prove that exact multiplier has already appeared, but it also did not establish absence. Its novelty remains unresolved, its causal explanation is inconsistent, and its raw evidence pointer says `none`.

### F-floor / memory-reset family
F/CR adaptation has a large literature, while dimension-level diversity intervention and stagnation-triggered reinitialization are explicit existing research directions. Candidate 1's formula does not implement the named per-dimension behavior. The combination therefore lacks a defensible novelty claim in its present form.

## What this matrix does and does not establish

- It establishes that the main building blocks proposed in Phase-1A-10 are established techniques.
- It does **not** prove that no exact combination has ever appeared.
- Exact absence claims require a broader paper-by-paper full-text and implementation audit.
- Because no candidate has passed that bar, the scientifically correct decision is to stop this candidate batch rather than spend API or performance budget.
